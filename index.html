Candidates

Two ways to hold the same inventory

Both keep AAN's platform navigation, the full row anatomy, light and dark themes. They differ in where the lot intelligence lives and how a vehicle opens.

Candidate A · Gen 10

Bento Command

Heavy-top overview · vehicle opens in place

The lot's intelligence sits above the list as a bento of unequal weight — one dominant Lot object, a Value module, a Needs-attention queue — each a dark tile lit from a different edge. The inventory runs full width beneath it. A vehicle never leaves the list: its row turns into a tab and a detail sheet unfolds under it, with the deeper sections as an accordion.

  • Bento header — Lot · Value · Attention as weighted tiles
  • Inline detail — accordion sheet under the row, no side panel
  • Search-first band — one wide field, sticks under the nav
  • Page scroll — sticky band and column header, edge to edge
  • Full row — Stock · Year · Make · Model · Trim · Colour · Price · Status · Health · Age · Actions
  • Attention as filters — tap a queue item, the list narrows
  • Price modes — MSRP saving, lease, call for price ↓

Candidate B · Gen 9

Context Plane

One plane that switches · the list never reflows

Built for exotic and supercar dealers on the same functional scope. The lot intelligence, the selected vehicle and the filters share one context plane beside the list: selecting a vehicle swaps the plane's content instead of pushing the table around, so the list stays exactly where the eye left it. Analytics live inside the workflow — lot value and aging in the Lot plane, comparable market in the Vehicle plane.

  • Context plane — Lot · Vehicle · Filters in one place
  • Zero reflow — the list keeps its position and width
  • Market in context — comps and pricing next to the car
  • Facet counts — filters show how many cars each choice leaves
  • Exotic tone — quieter chrome, larger photography
  • Gen 8 language — same platform bar, rows and themes

Open questions

Price — what a single field cannot say

Two questions the current page leaves open, with a proposal and the rule set. Gen 10 already renders every case on demo cars.

Question 1

Lease price

Some dealers list a monthly lease next to the asking price; most don't, and a car can have either, both or neither. Where does it live, and how do we show it without stretching every row for the few that have one?

Proposal

  • Lease is a qualifier, not a second price. The asking price stays the one figure in the Price column; the lease sits under it as a quiet 11.5px line — $2,450/mo with a small LEASE mark — only on cars that have one.
  • Rows keep one height. The 74px row already has room for a primary figure plus up to two qualifier lines; nothing reflows when a lease appears.
  • The vehicle sheet spells it out. Under the stats, a Pricing block lists MSRP, Discount and Lease as label / value rows — the place for the full numbers and, later, the term and down payment.
  • Filterable. "Has lease price" becomes a facet in Merchandising, next to "Has photos", so a lease campaign can be worked as a list.
  • Feeds. Lease goes out as its own field, never concatenated into the price string.

Rules

lease = null
Nothing shown. No placeholder, no empty line.
lease > 0, price set
Price first, lease line under it. Sort and price facets use the asking price only.
lease > 0, no price
Price cell shows — and the lease line; Health still flags NO PRICE. A lease alone does not satisfy the price check.
Filters open
Qualifier lines hide (narrow list); the sheet still has them.

Question 2

MSRP, "OFF", text in the price field

Clients type things into the price that are not an asking price: the MSRP itself, "$5,000 OFF", "Call", "Sale". Today it is one free field, so the list, the feeds and the filters cannot tell them apart. How do we show what the client meant without changing what they typed?

Proposal

  • One price, plus a price mode. The field stays numeric; a small mode next to it says what the number is: Asking (default) · MSRP · Call for price. Anything a client types that is not a number is parsed into a mode on save — "MSRP" → MSRP, "Call" → Call for price — and the client sees the mode we picked.
  • MSRP is its own field. When both MSRP and asking price exist, the row shows the asking price with the MSRP struck out and the saving in green: $100,800 · $118,500 −$17.7K. "OFF" is never typed — it is computed.
  • MSRP-as-price is marked, not hidden. If the client's number is the MSRP, the row shows it with a small MSRP tag, so nobody mistakes a sticker price for a deal price.
  • "Call for price" is a decision, not an error. The cell shows a quiet Call for price pill; Health shows OK, not NO PRICE. Feeds send the call-for-price flag instead of a number.
  • The Health column stays honest. NO PRICE means the client has not decided; Call for price means they have. Two different queues for the manager.

Rules

mode = asking
Number as today. If MSRP is set and higher: struck MSRP + saving under it.
mode = msrp
Number with an MSRP tag. Sorting and ranges treat it as a price; feeds send it as MSRP.
mode = call
Call for price pill, no number. Health OK. Excluded from price ranges; sorts after priced cars.
typed "5,000 OFF"
Stored as discount only if MSRP exists (price = MSRP − 5,000); otherwise rejected with a hint to set MSRP first.
typed text
Never shown raw in the list. Unrecognised text is kept in the record and surfaced in the sheet as "Price note".

Nothing removedEvery column, action, lane and state of the current page is present in every generation.
